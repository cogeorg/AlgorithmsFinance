The two horizons have different initial dates and strikes: they are separate
contracts. Exercise payoffs are gross K-P_t, rather than profits after premiums.
UTC dates use the supplied hourly bar labels; each price is that bar's close.

one_year: 36/40 early exercises; 4/40 never exercise.
  Never: NEARUSDT, TRXUSDT, UNIUSDT, ZECUSDT
full_history: 36/40 early exercises; 4/40 never exercise.
  Never: BNBUSDT, PAXGUSDT, TRXUSDT, XRPUSDT

Full-history data convention:
  All 43,823 observed closes are retained, giving N=43,822.
  One hour is missing: 2023-03-24 13:00 UTC; one observed interval spans 2 h.
  Actual elapsed time: 43823 h; ACT/365F T=5.002625570776256.
  Mean model period: 1.0000228195883347 h = elapsed time/N.
  The approved observed-interval approximation uses sample volatility of
  observed returns and R=exp(0.04*T/N), without filling or removing prices.
  Every affected row is labelled observed_intervals_approximation.
  The original strict hourly gate still rejects this window by default.

Interpretation and scaling:
  Calibration uses each entire historical window. This is a retrospective
  lab experiment, rather than an out-of-sample investment backtest.
  The rule finds the first crossing of the model boundary; it does not
  choose the largest realized future payoff after seeing the whole path.
  The NumPy engine evaluates the same recurrence on one date's row at a time.
  Its small trees match exact rational decisions; the real Bitcoin one-year
  price, all 8,761 boundaries and first exercise match the reference engine.
  For 40 assets, full-history pricing took 24.13 times the one-year pricing time.
  Time remains O(M*N^2); rolling-row storage is O(N), plus an O(M) results
  table. Memory did not bind within the assigned horizons and asset count.
