# One-year CPU worker scaling

The 40 instructor-supplied crypto datasets were processed with Version B using 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 16 workers, three sequential batches each. A single asset was warmed up before all measurements. Wall times include loading, calibration, process startup, pricing and result collection. All 21 batches completed, each with 40 assets; prices matched the sequential baseline within 0.0001 and parameters and period counts matched exactly. Input hashes were unchanged.

The fastest observed median was 12 workers, 3.459 seconds, speedup 4.821x and efficiency 40.2%. Eight workers achieved 3.498 seconds, only 0.038 seconds slower. At 16 workers the median rose to 3.659 seconds. Marginal speedup per added worker declined from 0.538 between 2 and 4 workers to 0.013 between 8 and 12, then became negative between 12 and 16. The 2-worker efficiency slightly above 100% is a measured result, not evidence of guaranteed superlinear scaling. These are three-run medians in one fixed configuration order; startup, caching, system activity and NumPy threading may affect comparisons.

`summary.csv` defines speedup as the sequential median divided by the worker median, efficiency as speedup/workers, and marginal time saving and speedup using consecutive worker counts. `empirical_speedup_curvature` is the difference between successive marginal speedup-per-worker slopes divided by the distance between their interval midpoints. It is an empirical finite difference, not option gamma. Initial intervals without enough observations are blank.

The recorded machine was Intel Core Ultra 9 285H, 16 available logical CPUs, Windows 11, Python 3.14.6 and NumPy 2.5.5. Raw repetitions and per-asset comparisons, environment and source hashes, summary and chart are in `40_Results/cpu_scaling_one_year/`. Previous 1/2/4-worker results remain in `40_Results/parallel_one_year/` as historical references. To rerun from the lab folder: `python -B 20_Code/cpu_scaling.py`; the existing result folder must first be preserved or moved since the script refuses to overwrite it.
